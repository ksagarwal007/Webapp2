    How the calculator works

    Both choices start with the same loan and the same extra money, and run month by month until your loan's original end date.

    • Prepay: the extra goes into the loan every month. Your EMI stays the same, so the loan closes sooner. From then on, your EMI and the extra are invested every month until the original end date.
    • Invest: the loan runs its normal course, and the extra is invested every month from day one.

    At the end, the loan is fully repaid either way, so the choice with the bigger investment pot after tax is the one that leaves you richer. The breakeven return is the yearly return at which both choices come out equal.

    Tax rules used, tax year 2026-27

    • Home loan interest, old regime: up to ₹2,00,000 a year for a self-occupied home, under section 22 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (section 24(b) of the 1961 Act). The tax saved each year is assumed to be invested.
    • Home loan interest, new regime: no deduction for a self-occupied home.
    • Equity mutual funds: long-term gains taxed at 12.5% above ₹1,25,000 a year, plus 4% cess. Budget 2026 left these rates unchanged.
    • Debt funds: gains taxed at your income tax slab rate, plus 4% cess, for units bought on or after 1 April 2023.
    • FDs: interest taxed every year at your slab rate, plus 4% cess.

    We leave out surcharge, prepayment charges (banks can't charge them on floating-rate home loans taken by individuals; on a fixed-rate loan, check your loan terms) and the old-regime deduction for principal repayment, which most people already use up through PF and other savings.

    When prepaying makes more sense

    • You're on the new tax regime, so interest gives you no tax benefit.
    • Your loan rate is high, or you'd invest in FDs or debt funds rather than equity.
    • You have less than about five years left, too short to ride out market ups and downs.
    • Being debt-free sooner matters to you, for peace of mind or a planned career break.

    When investing makes more sense

    • You have many years left and invest in equity funds steadily.
    • You're on the old regime and still claim the full ₹2,00,000 interest deduction.
    • You already have an emergency fund and adequate insurance.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is it better to prepay a home loan or invest?

    It depends mainly on three things: your loan's interest rate, the return you expect after tax, and whether you get a tax deduction on the interest. If your investments can beat the loan's effective interest rate after tax, investing usually wins over the long run. If not, prepaying wins, and it's guaranteed.

    Does prepaying make sense under the new tax regime?

    Under the new regime you get no deduction for interest on a self-occupied home, so every rupee of interest you avoid by prepaying is a real saving. That makes prepaying relatively more attractive than under the old regime.

    How does the old regime change the answer?

    Under the old regime you can deduct up to ₹2,00,000 of home loan interest a year on a self-occupied home (section 22 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, earlier section 24(b)). Prepaying reduces your interest, so it also reduces this tax saving. The calculator includes that lost saving.

    Should I reduce my EMI or my tenure when I prepay?

    This calculator assumes you keep the same EMI and shorten the tenure, which saves the most interest. Once the loan closes, it assumes you invest your EMI and the extra amount every month until the original end date, so both choices are compared over the same period.

    How are investment returns taxed in the calculator?

    Equity mutual fund gains are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1,25,000, plus 4% cess, when you sell at the end. Debt fund gains are taxed at your slab rate when you sell (for units bought on or after 1 April 2023). FD interest is taxed every year at your slab rate, so FD money grows at the after-tax rate.

    Is the result guaranteed?

    No. Prepaying gives a certain saving, while market returns are not guaranteed and can be lower than you expect, especially over short periods. Use the breakeven return to judge how much risk you're taking: if you need a high return for investing to win, prepaying is the safer choice.
