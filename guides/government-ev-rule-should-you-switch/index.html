Guide · Your car

The government will pay twice as much for an EV. Would it pay off for you?

From 10 November 2026, electric cars are the default for central government staff cars, with a ₹20 lakh price ceiling against ₹10 lakh for petrol. Would paying twice as much for an EV pay off for you?

By Salarist

Published 15 Nov 2026Office Memorandum, 10 Nov 20264 min read

In this guide

  1. The short answer
  2. What the government ordered
  3. ₹20 lakh EV vs ₹10 lakh petrol
  4. What it means for you
  5. What the government's sums don't face

The short answer

For an official car driven 2,000–3,000 km a month, a ₹20 lakh EV is far cheaper over its life than a ₹10 lakh petrol car. For you, it depends on distance. Counting the price, running costs and resale, a car costing twice as much breaks even at about 1,526 km a month over 7 years, or 1,257 km over 10. At a typical 1,000 km a month, petrol still costs less. A smaller price gap moves the break-even down a lot.

Compare an EV with your current car

What the government ordered

  • Who: central government ministries and departments, for staff cars, when existing petrol or diesel cars are due for replacement. Not a ban, and not an immediate switch.
  • EVs by default: "the default and preferred choice", with a price ceiling (net dealer price) of ₹20 lakh, against ₹10 lakh for petrol, diesel and hybrid cars.
  • Petrol or diesel only with approval: a justified proposal to the Department of Expenditure, approved by the department's Secretary and its Financial Adviser. The same applies to hiring one.
  • Why: electric mobility, less fossil fuel, and lifecycle costs. In 2022, EVs were about 0.6% of government vehicles.

₹20 lakh EV vs ₹10 lakh petrol

These are net costs: what each car costs you over the years, not its price. Net cost = the price you pay, plus charging or fuel, insurance and servicing, minus what you get back when you sell it. Paid in cash; EV figure first:

How the numbers add up: 7 years

Petrol, 1,000 km a month₹10 L+ ₹6.3 L+ ₹1.7 L+ ₹76,625− ₹4 L= ₹14.7 L
EV, 2,000 km a month₹20 L+ ₹2.1 L+ ₹3 L+ ₹38,312− ₹7 L= ₹18.5 L
Petrol, 2,000 km a month₹10 L+ ₹12.5 L+ ₹1.7 L+ ₹76,625− ₹4 L= ₹21 L

So the ₹20 lakh EV costs you about ₹17.4 L over 7 years at 1,000 km a month: you pay ₹20 lakh, spend about ₹4.4 L running it, and get about ₹7 L back when you sell.

Running cost is what makes the difference: about ₹1.1 a km for the EV charged at home, against ₹6.8 a km for petrol.

What it means for you

  • Drive a lot, or keep cars long? An EV wins even at twice the price.
  • Typical city use (about 1,000 km a month)? An EV pays off only if the price gap is smaller. Most personal buyers compare an EV with its petrol version, not one at half the price. See Petrol, CNG or EV: cost per km.
  • Through your employer: a leased EV is taxed as a perquisite at the small-car rate (₹5,000 a month when also used privately), whatever its price, which can tip the sums. See Company car perquisite value.

What the government's sums don't face

  • Charging at home: the low running cost assumes a home or office charger; public fast charging can cost three or four times as much.
  • Resale: used-EV prices are still settling. We've assumed EVs keep a little less value than petrol cars.
  • The battery: warranties often run 8 years; a replacement after that is expensive.

How these numbers were worked out

From the car ownership cost calculator's own code. EV: ₹20 lakh, 7 km a unit at ₹8, insurance ₹60,000 then ₹40,000, servicing ₹5,000 a year, worth 35% after 7 years or 20% after 10. Petrol: ₹10 lakh, 15 km a litre at ₹102.12 (Delhi, 14 November 2026), insurance ₹35,000 then ₹22,000, servicing ₹10,000 a year, worth 40% or 25%. Costs rise 3% a year. Source: Department of Expenditure Office Memorandum F. No. 16(17)/E.Coord/2025, 10 November 2026 (text via StaffNews; reported by The Federal, Free Press Journal and Cartoq).
