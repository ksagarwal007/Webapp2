Petrol, CNG or EV: what a car really costs per km

The fuel bill is the cost you notice, but it's often not the biggest one. Here's what a petrol, CNG and electric car really cost per km once you add the loan, insurance, service and the value the car loses, worked out with the Salarist car ownership cost calculator.

By Salarist

Published 14 Nov 2026Fuel prices of 14 Nov 20264 min read

The short answer

Driving 1,200 km a month and keeping the car 7 years, the petrol car costs about ₹17.77 per km all in, the CNG version about ₹15.43 and the electric car about ₹15.62. CNG pays off from very low distances; the EV needs more kilometres to make up for its higher price, but wins clearly the more you drive.

Compare your own cars

Three versions of the same car

Take a compact car, bought with an 80% loan at 9% for 5 years, driven 1,200 km a month, and sold after 7 years:

  • Petrol: ₹10 L on-road, 15 km a litre, petrol at ₹102.12 (Delhi, 14 Nov 2026). Sold for 40% of the price.
  • CNG: ₹10.9 L on-road (about ₹90,000 more), 24 km a kg, CNG at ₹86.98 a kg. Sold for 38%.
  • Electric: ₹14 L on-road, 7 km a unit, home charging at ₹8 a unit. Sold for 35%.

Prices, mileage, insurance and resale values here are examples. Put in your own in the calculator.

The less you drive, the more the fixed costs (price, insurance, loan interest, value lost) dominate, and the cheapest car to buy wins. The more you drive, the more fuel matters, and the cheapest car to run wins. Fuel alone costs ₹6.81 per km for petrol, ₹3.62 for CNG and ₹1.14 for home charging.

For the electric car, the value it loses (₹9.1 L) is about 7 times its charging bill (₹1.3 L). That's why the resale value you assume matters so much: EV resale values are still less settled than petrol's, so try a lower figure to see how much it changes.

The break-even distances

  • Petrol vs CNG: CNG is cheaper above about 400 km a month.
  • Petrol vs electric: the EV is cheaper above about 800 km a month. At 1,200 km, it's cheaper only if you keep it 4 years or more.
  • CNG vs electric: the EV beats CNG above about 1,300 km a month.

Which one suits you

  • You drive little (under about 500 km a month): buy the cheapest car you're happy with. Fuel is a small part of the cost.
  • Daily city commute (1,000 to 1,500 km): CNG and electric both beat petrol; it comes down to the price gap, CNG station queues and boot space versus home charging.
  • Long daily drives (2,000 km and more): an EV saves the most, if you can charge at home and the range fits your trips.
  • Paying cash? Without the loan, every option costs less (petrol ₹15.9 L, CNG ₹13.4 L, electric ₹13 L), and the pricier cars gain most because there's no interest on the bigger loan.

How these numbers were worked out

All figures come from the car ownership cost calculator: 7 years, fuel and service costs rising 3% a year, insurance at the first-year premium and then a renewal estimate, resale falling by the same share each year. Fuel prices are Delhi prices on 14 November 2026. Personal car-loan interest isn't tax-deductible for salaried people. Read how we check our numbers.
