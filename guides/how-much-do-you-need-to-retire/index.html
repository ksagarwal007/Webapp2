How much do you need to retire? One person's numbers

Most people know their retirement number only as a vague "a few crore". Here's how to work out yours, through one person's numbers with the Salarist retirement planner: what she'll need, what her EPF and SIPs will add up to, and what closes the gap.

By Salarist

Published 14 Nov 2026EPF 8.25% for 2025-26 · before tax4 min read

The short answer

A 35-year-old who spends ₹60,000 a month today and wants that lifestyle from 60 to 85 needs about ₹6.76 Cr at 60 (about ₹1.57 Cr in today's money), after her EPS pension. On her current EPF and SIPs she'll have about ₹5.62 Cr. An extra SIP of about ₹5,343 a month, rising 5% a year, closes the gap; so does retiring at 62 or spending ₹50,000 instead of ₹60,000.

Work out your own number

Meera's plan

  • Meera is 35 and wants to retire at 60, with money lasting until 85.
  • She spends ₹60,000 a month today, and expects to spend the same in retirement (in today's money).
  • She has ₹6 lakh in EPF on PF wages of ₹50,000 a month (rising 6% a year), and 8 years in EPS.
  • She has ₹8 lakh in mutual funds and a SIP of ₹10,000 a month that she raises 5% a year, plus ₹3 lakh in FDs and PPF.
  • Assumptions: 6% inflation, 11% on mutual funds, 7% on FDs, and 7% on her savings after she retires.

What she needs

  • Spending at 60: ₹60,000 today becomes about ₹2,57,512 a month after 25 years of 6% inflation.
  • For 25 years: that spending keeps rising every year she's retired, while her savings earn 7%.
  • Less her EPS pension: about ₹10,643 a month from 60. It doesn't rise with inflation, so it covers less each year.
  • Result: she needs about ₹6.76 Cr at 60.

Without changes, her money would run out at about 80.

Closing the gap

ChangeResult
Extra SIP of ₹5,343 a month, rising 5% a yearGap closed
Retire at 62 instead of 60Gap down to ₹6.2 L
Spend ₹50,000 a month instead of ₹60,000On track
Put ₹5,000 a month into NPSGap down to ₹24.5 L, plus an NPS pension of about ₹10,119 a month

The extra SIP is the simplest: starting it at 35, with yearly increases, costs much less than catching up at 45.

The assumptions that matter most

  • Inflation: at 7% instead of 6%, she'd need ₹9.61 Cr and the extra SIP would be ₹18,662 a month.
  • Returns: 1% lower on everything takes the extra SIP to ₹13,190 a month.
  • How long you plan for: planning to 90 instead of 85 takes the need to ₹7.95 Cr. Running out at 85 is worse than leaving some behind.
  • Tax: these are before-tax figures. EPF withdrawals are tax-free, but pensions, FD interest and part of mutual fund gains are taxed, so keep a margin.

How these numbers were worked out

All figures come from the retirement planner, which uses the EPF calculator's and NPS calculator's own code: EPF at 8.25% (the rate approved for 2025-26, assumed to stay the same), the EPS pension estimate under EPS 2026, SIPs growing monthly and stepping up yearly, and withdrawals at the start of each retirement year.
