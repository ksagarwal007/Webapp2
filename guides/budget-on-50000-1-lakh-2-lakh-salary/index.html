Budgeting on ₹50,000, ₹1 lakh or ₹2 lakh a month: three example budgets

There's no single right budget for a salary. What changes as income grows is where the pressure sits: rent at ₹50,000, a child's costs at ₹1 lakh, and the home loan and school fees at ₹2 lakh. Here are three example budgets, worked out with the Salarist budget planner, and what each household should fix first.

The short answer

All three households below keep their needs, wants and savings in check, but the share they save doesn't rise with income. Rohan saves 26% of ₹50,000; the Iyers save 20% of ₹2 lakh. As pay grows, so do the fixed commitments: a bigger home, a loan, school fees. A higher salary only builds wealth if savings grow with it.

₹50,000 a month: Rohan

Rohan is 26, single, and shares a 2BHK in Pune. He sends ₹4,000 a month home to his parents and rides a bike to work. He has ₹60,000 saved for emergencies.

What the three budgets have in common

  1. Needs above 50% are normal in a big Indian city. The 50/30/20 rule is a starting point; a better test is whether you save 20% or more in all.
  2. Every one of them is short on the emergency fund. It's the first thing to build, at any salary.
  3. Yearly costs need a monthly set-aside, from ₹4,000 a month for Rohan to ₹39,417 for the Iyers.
  4. Raise savings with every raise. Increase your SIP when your pay goes up, before the extra money finds somewhere else to go.

How these numbers were worked out

Every figure comes from the same calculation as the budget planner, run on each household's numbers. Yearly amounts are divided by 12, and daily amounts are multiplied by 365 and divided by 12. The emergency fund target is six months of needs, including EMIs. The households are examples, not averages or survey data; costs vary a lot by city and family. The planner works with in-hand pay, after tax, so no tax rules apply. Read how we check our numbers.
