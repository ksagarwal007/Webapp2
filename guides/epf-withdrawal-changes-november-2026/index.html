EPF withdrawal rules are changing: what the new decisions mean for you

On 19 November 2026, EPFO's board approved three big changes to EPF withdrawals: more of your balance, a shorter wait after a job loss, and regular payouts for retirees. None of them apply yet. Here's what changes, with one member's numbers.

By Salarist

The short answer

Once notified, you'll be able to withdraw up to 90% of your EPF instead of 75%, take out the whole balance 2 months after losing a job instead of 12, and, on retiring, take your EPF as monthly, quarterly or half-yearly payouts while the rest keeps earning interest. For Asha, 30, with ₹6 lakh in EPF, the most she can withdraw rises from ₹4.5 L to ₹5.4 L. Until the changes are notified, today's rules apply.

The decisions were taken at the 240th meeting of the Central Board of Trustees (CBT), chaired by the Labour Minister. The three withdrawal categories (essential needs, housing, special circumstances) and the 12 months' membership needed for partial withdrawals aren't changing. What it means for Asha

Asha's ₹6 lakhTodayOnce notified
Must stay in EPF₹1.5 L₹60,000
Most she can withdraw₹4.5 L₹5.4 L
What that amount would have grown to by 58₹41.4 L₹49.7 L

The extra ₹90,000 she could take out would have grown to about ₹8.3 L by 58 at 8.25% a year. More access is useful in a real emergency, but every rupee withdrawn stops compounding at one of the best safe rates available.

If you lose your job

Today, a member who stops working has to wait 12 months to settle the whole EPF balance (partial withdrawals are still allowed). The approved change brings this down to 2 months, close to the old rule before the EPF Scheme, 2026. The pension part (EPS) still needs 36 months out of work, and only with under 10 years of service: see Withdrawal benefit or scheme certificate? If you're only changing jobs, transfer, don't withdraw: Changed jobs? Transfer your PF.

Monthly payouts after retiring

Today you take your EPF as one lump sum when you retire. Under the approved Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP), you could instead take it in instalments while the rest stays in EPF earning interest. It's voluntary, and if the member dies during the payouts, the nominee can take the rest or carry on with the instalments.

If Asha left her ₹6 lakh untouched, it would be about ₹55.2 L at 58 at 8.25% a year, before any new contributions. As an illustration, that could pay about ₹46,033 a month for 20 years, or ₹42,453 for 25. Taking ₹40,000 a month, it would last about 31 years. EPFO hasn't yet published how the payouts will be worked out, and the tax treatment depends on an Income-tax Act amendment that's still with the Finance Ministry. Read Annuity or SWP for how payouts compare with a pension.

When it starts

Not yet. The board's approval doesn't change the rules by itself: the EPF Scheme, 2026 has to be amended and notified first, and the payout option also needs a change in income tax law. No date has been announced. Until then, the 25% minimum balance and the 12-month wait still apply. We'll update this page when the notification is out.

What to do now

  1. Don't plan around the new limits yet, if you need money soon: today's 75% and 12-month rules apply.
  2. Out of work and waiting? Partial withdrawals are already allowed; the full balance waits for the notification or 12 months.
  3. Near retirement? Think about how much you'd want as a lump sum and how much as payouts. See How much will your EPF be at 58?
