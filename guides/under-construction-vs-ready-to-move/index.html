Under construction or ready to move? The real cost of waiting for your flat

An under-construction flat is often cheaper on paper. But until you get the keys, you pay interest on the loan and rent on the home you live in, at the same time. Here's how much that really costs, and how much cheaper the flat needs to be to make up for it.

By Salarist

Published 13 Nov 2026Written for tax year 2026-274 min read

The short answer

While the home is being built, you pay pre-EMI interest on the loan and rent together. For a ₹90 L flat with possession in 3 years, that's about ₹18.4 L of interest and ₹9.1 L of rent. To come out level with a ready-to-move flat, the under-construction one needs to be about 16% cheaper in this example, and every year of delay makes it worse.

Compare your options

The example

We use the same buyer as our main rent or buy guide: Arjun in Pune, looking at a ₹90 L flat with 20% down and a loan at 8.5% for 20 years. He pays ₹24,000 a month in rent today and plans to stay 12 years. We compare buying the flat ready to move in with buying it under construction at the same price.

What the wait costs

With the full loan paid out at the start, here's what Arjun pays before possession, and how much less he ends up with after 12 years than if he'd bought ready to move in:

2 years₹5.9 L₹12.2 L₹4 L less
3 years₹9.1 L₹18.4 L₹7.4 L less
4 years₹12.4 L₹24.5 L₹10.4 L less

Each extra year of waiting costs him roughly another ₹3.1 L by the end. That's why a builder's delay hurts so much: you keep paying rent and interest, and the EMI that actually reduces the loan starts later.

How much cheaper should it be?

For Arjun's numbers, an under-construction flat with possession in 3 years needs to cost about ₹76 L, roughly 16% less than the ready flat, for buying it to come out as well as buying ready. If the discount is smaller, the ready flat is the better buy on money alone.

Your figure will differ with your rent, loan rate and the years to possession. Choose "Under construction" in the calculator and try different prices.

Other things to weigh

  • Delays. Add a year or two to the builder's date when you run the numbers. Check the project's registration and progress on your state's RERA website.
  • GST. Under-construction homes attract GST, which a ready home with a completion certificate doesn't. Ask the builder for the full cost sheet and put the GST in "Other buying costs".
  • Loan paid out in stages. Many loans are paid out as construction progresses, so early interest is lower than our full-payout assumption. Ask your lender for the schedule.
  • Tax, old regime only. Interest paid before the year of possession can be claimed in five equal parts from that year, within the ₹2,00,000 yearly limit. If possession comes more than five years after the loan year, the limit drops to ₹30,000.
  • The upside. A new flat, a better choice of units, and sometimes faster price growth in a new area.

How these numbers were worked out

All figures come from the Rent or Buy calculator, run on Arjun's inputs: prices grow 6% a year, he'd invest the difference in equity funds at 12%, the full loan is paid out at the start with interest-only payments until possession, and he rents until he moves in. The "how much cheaper" figure keeps the down payment at 20% and stamp duty at 7% of the lower price. Tax rules are for tax year 2026-27. Read how we check our numbers.
