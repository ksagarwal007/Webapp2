Step-up SIP: how a 10% yearly increase changes your final amount

Most people start a SIP and leave it at the same amount for years, even as their pay rises. A step-up raises it a little every year. Here's how much difference that makes, worked out with the Salarist SIP calculator.

By Salarist

The short answer

₹10,000 a month for 20 years at 12% a year grows to about ₹92 L. Raise it by 10% every year and it grows to about ₹1.86 Cr, roughly twice as much. To get there without a step-up, you'd need to start at about ₹20,300 a month.

Try a step-up

What a step-up SIP is

A step-up (or top-up) SIP increases your monthly amount by a fixed percentage once a year. With ₹10,000 and a 10% step-up, you invest ₹10,000 a month in year 1, ₹11,000 in year 2, ₹12,100 in year 3, and so on. Most fund houses and apps let you set this once when you start.

The numbers over 20 years

Starting at ₹10,000 a month, 12% a year:

Step-upSIP in year 20Total put inValue after 20 years
0% a year₹10,000₹24 L₹92 L
5% a year₹25,270₹39.7 L₹1.28 Cr
10% a year₹61,159₹68.7 L₹1.86 Cr
15% a year₹1,42,318₹1.23 Cr₹2.86 Cr

Why it adds so much

Part of the difference is simply that you put in more: ₹68.7 L with a 10% step-up, against ₹24 L without. But it's also painless, because each year's increase comes out of a pay rise, not out of today's budget. You never feel the full ₹61,159 a month you're investing by year 20.

How much to step up

  • Match your pay rise, not more. If your salary grows 8–10% a year, a 10% step-up keeps your savings rate steady.
  • Be careful with big step-ups. At 15% a year, the SIP reaches ₹1,42,318 a month by year 20. That only works if your income keeps up.
  • Skip a year if you need to. Most platforms let you pause or change the step-up. A smaller increase is better than stopping the SIP.

How to set it up

  1. In your fund app, look for "step-up", "top-up" or "SIP increase" when you create or edit a SIP.
  2. Choose a percentage or a fixed amount, and the month it rises (just after your appraisal works well).
  3. Check your plan in the SIP calculator, and see how much SIP you need for a goal.

How these numbers were worked out

All figures come from the SIP calculator: each SIP goes in at the start of the month, returns compound so a year gives exactly 12%, and the SIP rises once every 12 months. Values are before tax. Returns are assumptions, not predictions. Read how we check our numbers.
