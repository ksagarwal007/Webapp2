SIP or lump sum: what to do with your bonus

Got a Diwali or year-end bonus and want to invest it? You can put it all into a mutual fund now or spread it over a few months. Here's what each does, worked out with the Salarist SIP calculator.

By Salarist

Published 14 Nov 20263 min read

In this guide

  1. The short answer
  2. Before you invest it
  3. Lump sum vs spreading it out

The short answer

If the money is for a goal 7 years or more away, investing it all at once usually ends up with more, because it's invested for longer. Spreading it over 6 to 12 months costs a little on average but protects you from investing everything just before a fall. If a 20% drop in the first few months would make you stop investing, spread it.

Try it in the SIP calculator

Before you invest it

  1. Emergency fund: keep 3 to 6 months of expenses somewhere safe. If you don't have it, start there. The budget planner shows how many months you have.
  2. Expensive debt: clear credit card dues and personal loans first; they cost far more than funds are likely to earn.
  3. Home loan? Compare prepaying with investing in Prepay or Invest.
  4. Short goals: money needed within 3 years is better in an FD or debt fund than in equity.

Lump sum vs spreading it out

Say your bonus is ₹2,00,000 and you invest it in an equity fund for 10 years, with markets returning 12% a year on average:

How you invest ₹2,00,000After 10 years
All at once now₹6.2 L
Over 6 months₹6.1 L
Over 12 months₹5.9 L

With steady returns, the lump sum wins by ₹31,128 over spreading across 12 months, because on average your money sits uninvested for about 6 months. But markets don't move steadily.

When the market falls, or rises

Two illustrations, both ending the first year 12% up, then growing at 12% a year:

  • Markets fall 20% over the first 6 months, then recover: spreading over 12 months buys more units while prices are low. After 10 years: ₹6.8 L spread out, against ₹6.2 L all at once.
  • Markets rise 25% in 6 months, then give some back: spreading buys at higher prices. After 10 years: ₹5.4 L spread out, against ₹6.2 L all at once.

Nobody knows which one is coming. Spreading narrows the range of outcomes; investing at once gives you the better result more often. The real risk is the one in your head: if you'd panic and sell after a fall, spreading is the better choice for you.

A simple way to do it

  1. Park the bonus in a liquid or money market fund (or your savings account).
  2. Set up a systematic transfer (STP) or a monthly SIP from it into your equity fund over 6 to 12 months.
  3. Or split it: half now, the rest over the next 6 months.
  4. Add the bonus to your goal in the SIP calculator as money already invested, to see if your goal is still on track.

Selling later? Read How mutual fund gains are taxed.

How these numbers were worked out

The steady-return figures come from the SIP calculator's own code, at 12% a year compounded monthly, with SIPs at the start of each month, before tax. The fall and rise cases are illustrations of one possible path, not predictions. Read how we check our numbers.
