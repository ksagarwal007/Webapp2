The ₹25,000 EPF wage ceiling: what it changes for your PF, pension and take-home

From 17 September 2026, the EPF wage ceiling went up from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month, the first change since 2014. Depending on how your PF is worked out, it can lower your take-home, raise your PF, and raise your future pension. Here's what it means for you.

The short answer

  • Earn up to ₹25,000? EPF, EPS and EDLI are now compulsory for you.
  • PF worked out on capped wages? Your PF deduction goes up from ₹1,800 to ₹3,000 a month, so take-home falls by ₹1,200, and your employer puts in ₹1,200 more too.
  • In EPS? Up to ₹2,083 a month now goes to your pension instead of ₹1,250, and service from 17 September 2026 counts at up to ₹25,000 for your pension.

See the effect on your EPF

What the wage ceiling is

It's the monthly wage (basic + DA) used to decide who must be covered by EPF, EPS and EDLI, and the most on which contributions are compulsory and EPS is worked out. It was ₹15,000 from September 2014. The Union Cabinet approved ₹25,000 on 16 September 2026, effective from 17 September 2026, and it's notified under the Code on Social Security, 2020.

Who it affects

  • New joiners earning ₹15,000 to ₹25,000: now covered compulsorily.
  • Members whose PF is worked out on the ceiling: contributions go up.
  • EPS members: more goes to the pension; members in EPF only with wages up to ₹25,000 are brought into EPS.
  • PF on full wages above ₹25,000, not in EPS: little changes.

If your PF is on capped wages

A monthBefore (₹15,000)Now (₹25,000)
Your PF (12%)₹1,800₹3,000
Employer to EPS (8.33%)₹1,250₹2,082.50
Employer to EPF₹550₹917.50

Over a career, that adds up. A 30-year-old with ₹1.5 lakh in EPF whose PF stays at the ceiling for 28 years would retire with about ₹62.6 L at the ₹25,000 ceiling, against ₹43.1 L had it stayed at ₹15,000 (at 8.25%, wages not rising).

If your PF is on full wages

Your and your employer's 12% don't change. But if you're in EPS, the employer's EPS share rises from ₹1,250 to up to ₹2,083 a month, so about ₹833 a month less goes into your EPF balance and more into your pension.

Your pension

EPS pensions are worked out separately for each ceiling period: service up to 16 September 2026 at up to ₹15,000, and service after at up to ₹25,000. With 30 years of service, 10 before the change and 20 after (plus 2 years added for 20 years or more), the pension is about ₹10,000 a month, against ₹6,857 under the old ceiling. The full benefit builds up only as you add service, and your last 60 months' wages need to be at or above ₹25,000.

What to check

  1. Your September and October payslips: is PF worked out on ₹25,000 now, if it was capped before?
  2. Your EPFO passbook: is a pension contribution showing, if your wages are up to ₹25,000?
  3. Your take-home: if it fell, the extra is going into your PF and pension, not lost.
